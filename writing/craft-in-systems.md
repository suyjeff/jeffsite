---
title: "Craft in Systems"
date: "2026-03-30"
---

how does a deeply complex system, filled with checkpoint after checkpoint, ever establish a firmly well-crafted product? does it have to start from the very beginning? is it possible to develop over time? how does one fix a specific layer of the onion without peeling back everything before it? these are the questions that confound me as I realize we've been planning for the past few weeks on shipping things without the quality consideration.

when you're not in the driver's seat, it's difficult to control your destination. but it's not impossible. you might give directions, you might offer your opinions, you might be a disruptive passenger, and on. all of these things merely nudge your vehicle in the way you want, but they don't firmly change your destination. if you try too hard as a passenger to manipulate the vehicle, it's likely you'll crash and no one will get anywhere.

for some, this is the call of leadership. if your teams aren't following the path you think is ideal, some may attempt to take the reigns themselves. often, this is not a smooth or viable option. and in the complex map of a corporation, where every node has a thousand values to understand, it's often better to collaborate.

but how might we do this if those with the most control don't have the same priorities as you? how does one pursue a more well-crafted product if those with power do not hold the same values?

as with any form of persuasion, you can try to create a tiered argument and appeal to their values. a better designed integration tile may improve the user's ability to actually connect their integrations, it may result in a less error-prone setup, it may make other functions we wish to tangentially unveil more appealing, it may do a thousand things. the problem with this approach is that those with the most control over directives and priorities have already thought through these potential worlds. whether or not they agree that these are worthy pursuits, they have some understanding through their own perspective that other goals are more worthy.

to achieve better craft, we must look at the dependencies that exist within our team. luckily for us (for now), there are functions that only we can perform. without them, those with more control cannot achieve their own goals. at face value, things like craft and visual design might seem external to the overlap between team goals and our own capabilities. however, i challenge you to look at the true mechanisms here: you are entrusted with delivering designs. these designs will be used to build the very items that the team needs to achieve our goals. rather than treating craft as a nice-to-have that can be baked *with* the design deliverables, recognize the truth sitting right in front of you: the design deliverables must be well-crafted themselves.

this simple realization should tell you the path forward: do not deliver work that does not come with beautiful and thoughtful design. do not ship things which are not well-crafted. if the unit of your delivery is a design, ensure that every delivery is well-crafted. when this is inherent in your work and in your thinking, it will naturally arrive in the end product as they are the ones asking for your indivisible unit.

---
title: "Getting to Doing"
date: "2026-03-23"
---

the past few weeks have been a blur of planning, scoping, sketching, and other stages of work that can be broadly categorized as "pre-design". i don't want to do this for too long. increasingly, the shape of work is changing. with all of the new ability and agency that Claude Code and rapid accelerators of efficiency have given us, i've lost a bit of focus. beforehand, the work was straightforward and limited: i spend some time exploring and drafting designs, i share them, and then the rest of the iceberg is left to the engineers. but now, as a member of a platform and the team that drives said platform, it no longer feels satisfactory to leave the complexities to engineers. this is for a few reasons: first, i want to understand those complexities better; second, i can do more *about* those things with new tooling; and finally, i fear if i do not *do* more and take more risks, the importance of my role will decrease as those more literate in AI-driven creation find themselves more capable in roles they aren't specialized for. this can be summarized as: everyone can do more now, so i can do more, so i should do more.

the problem with being able to do more is that your focus hasn't narrowed. instead, i've vastly broadened the scope of what i can do and how i can do it. instead of relying on a product spec and a table of P0s, i can make changes to our product myself, i can investigate features myself, and i can design for a slightly larger scope with the ability to handle the difference myself (i hope). this is all great for my own capabilities and influence, except it doesn't mean i actually *do* more. it means i **can** do more, not that i'll know what to do.

as someone that is trying to find their own direction with the agency that i've been given, it can be tough to know if i'm doing the right thing or not. i often feel like i'm in a haze of capability, ability, and responsibility. i have broad directives that are pretty clear, i have sub-goals which i know will help me get there, but it's tough to know what to do and when whenever i don't have exclusive control over all work streams. i'm in a constant mode of synthesis–just trying to collect my thoughts and focus my vision on what's actually happening can take just as much time as creating the things which will actually help with my team's goals.

i don't want to be spending all of this time gathering thoughts, synthesizing docs/slack threads/claude output/meeting notes, and writing more personal notes and claude queries to help interpret my interpretations. it's too layered. there's too much.

the way i work best is with clarity and direct action items. right now, those action items happen to be things that aren't true deliverables, but are instead steps before the actual cold hard work is done. this is necessary to an extent...but how do i figure out when enough is enough? AI has a few ways to solve this: you can relentlessly delegate your work streams with agents, simply brute-forcing all of the pre-design items in loops until you feel exhausted; you can achieve quick convergence on something because AI can synthesize things for you and thus begin the actual work faster; if you can exist in a hazy dream-state, AI will let you both work on the dam, the confluence, and the water quality.

what i am currently struggling with is figuring out which path is best. even this can be complicated by the massive improvement in understanding and synthesis that something like claude allows for. how do you actually work better and not simply produce random artifacts faster? there is no true value in summaries and specs if they are cheap. in the past, those items were useful since they helped us understand the complete picture and thus improved our ability to produce the deliverable. now, they come virtually free and anytime you want, thus their value has tanked. so, it's OK if it takes five claude summaries to understand something because in the past, it would only take one (or maybe half) through your own mental struggles. things are more efficient, but your mind's understanding is the same. it's simply inflation, but perhaps the market has opened up a bit.

to find your focus, stop the summaries and planning once you can see the direction. start building the simple stuff and see if AI can't help you do **that** faster.

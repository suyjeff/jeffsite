The Eng BridgeBack

March 2026

the industry is clearly transitioning–to what, i'm not sure. it's been a while since an entire form of work has been so easily democratized and automated. much less a field of work that many spend years dedicating their formative years towards, only to find themselves lost and inexperienced relative to a sea of machines. i'm not talking about just software engineering, law, or financial analysis. i'm talking about the entirety of knowledge work. i'm talking about how all of the bureaucratic mess that composes parts of useful functions like law and finance have since been washed away into the mess of the machine. summaries, specs, presentations, messages, and on are caught in the spin cycle of anthropic's near-daily releases, a productivity-obsessed vocal-minority of workers, companies eager to ride an innovation wave, and another vocal-minority of near-luddites who resist the encroachment of AI into knowledge work. the only saving grace, in my eyes, is that my worth was never defined by my artifact output or code-generation. it's always been in details, thoughts, and once-in-a-while outputs driven by some part-creative part-analytical process. i don't deny that this could too become washed away in the data centers, but i also know that new capabilities will arise both in humans and in myself. in the meantime, i believe that the current bridge is one of understanding. in order to remain a useful, knowledgeable, agentic, and tasteful contributor, i need to bridge the gap in my mind that is tied to the actual creation of my designs.

there's a lot that goes into a piece of production-level software. there are thousands, if not millions of checks and balances that have existed long before cursor bugbot could do things while we sleep. there are endless standards long upheld in the engineering process which i simply do not understand. there are conventions, principles, and ways-of-acting which, at least in years past, come only through experience. all of these things build a resilient individual who can move confidently through their codebase (just as i can a canvas) and come out with a great product.

it's important to delineate the environments here. moving through a codebase and moving through a canvas are both useful things, both are necessary to create something good, and both are independent skills which defined a decade of strong output. but, when a mysterious force enters the equation and drastically improves the efficiency and ability of one environment–the one most closely tied to the output of my particular industry–something has to change. and things did change: engineers became drastically more efficient and in some cases, much more able. they did this by building upon a set of capabilities and understandings of the code world, and now spend less time mired in the process pieces of writing PRs, thinking on their keyboards about functions, writing repetitive code. the pipeline to their output became both wider (as more freeform inputs are allowed) and much much shorter.

for designers, the same isn't quite true. while we can move through the canvas slightly quicker, that was never the true challenge. ideation can be arduous, but it is the wrestling of ideas and the exploration of tangents which is design and leads to the better output–not speed. now, a designer can drastically speed-up their ability to output things as well, but not necessarily output something good. design is still a process of thinking on canvas. that canvas might change to a code-backed one, or it might exist entirely on the web itself, or it might exist entirely in the computer's interpretation of the code which they can now edit without a sweat. regardless, the designer is now much closer to the output.

this new world is only useful for the designer if they can also act with an understanding of the pipeline and the output which gives them agentic freedom. what good is being able to code or create a prototype if one can not do so in a way that creates an actual, useable artifact?

currently, i exist in a fog when browsing through our codebase. i understand the basic language, the common parlance, and some conventions. but i do not understand things in the way i can quickly grasp the structure and definitions of a design system or product. as a result, i do not move fluidly through our codebase, i move with blurry eyes and a trusty machine instructing me on where is safe and where is not. i suspect this will always be the case for those that begin their exploration of code assisted by the machine. but, it cannot be this way forever. in order for me, the designer, to feel comfortable traversing a filesystem and comfortable skating through the canvas, i need to know the source.

fundamentally, i cannot do things that affect our bottom line (production code) without the confidence that i'm doing the right things. i have confidence, but i also take only extremely calculated risks. perhaps this is not always a good thing, but when my actions can have this many downstream consequences, i'll take the hit. in order for me to move more swiftly, i need to know that my actions are both safe and rational. i need to have some innate sense of the web of terms, conventions, precautions, tools, techniques, and judgement that allows engineers to understand the world through the filesystem.

this will take a long time–at least, longer than i think. the ironic part is that i believe the machines may help me get there. they'll read this document and understand my fears and desires, and through it, hopefully help me reach a level of understanding that can help me cross "the eng bridge".

this may all become a moot point as we move closer to "a country of geniuses in a datacenter". but in the meantime, and as an investment in the post-machine world, i think this is a useful bridge to create.
